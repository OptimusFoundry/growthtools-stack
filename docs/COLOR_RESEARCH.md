## 2. Color Palette Systems

### Monochromatic Palettes

**Rule:** One base hue with variations in lightness (tints) and darkness (shades).

**When to use:** Elegant, clean, minimal designs. Brand-focused content where you want cohesion without distraction. Works well for professional/corporate video ads.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue** | `#E3F2FD` | `#90CAF9` | `#42A5F5` | `#1565C0` | `#0D47A1` |
| **Green** | `#E8F5E9` | `#A5D6A7` | `#66BB6A` | `#2E7D32` | `#1B5E20` |

### Analogous Palettes

**Rule:** Three adjacent colors on the color wheel (within 30-60 degrees of each other).

**When to use:** Harmonious, serene designs. Nature scenes, wellness, flowing content. Low visual tension.

| Name | Color 1 | Color 2 | Color 3 | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunset Warm** | `#FF6B35` | `#FF8C42` | `#FFB347` | Energetic warmth |
| **Ocean Blues** | `#0077B6` | `#00B4D8` | `#90E0EF` | Calm, aquatic |

| **Berry** | `#7B2D8E` | `#C2185B` | `#E91E63` | Rich, luxurious |

| **Lavender Fields** | `#7C4DFF` | `#B388FF` | `#EA80FC` | Soft, creative |

### Complementary Palettes

**Rule:** Two colors directly opposite on the color wheel (180 degrees apart). Maximum contrast.

**When to use:** High-impact ads, CTAs, bold statements. Creates strong visual tension. Use one as dominant (60%), the other as accent (10%).

| Pair Name | Color A | Color B | Use Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Teal & Orange** | `#008B8B` | `#FF6B35` | Cinematic, blockbuster look |
| **Blue & Orange** | `#1565C0` | `#FF8F00` | Tech + energy, presentation |
| **Purple & Yellow** | `#7B1FA2` | `#FDD835` | Creative, attention-grabbing |
| **Red & Cyan** | `#D32F2F` | `#00BCD4` | Bold, high-energy |
| **Green & Magenta** | `#2E7D32` | `#E91E63` | Nature meets vibrance |
| **Navy & Gold** | `#0D1B2A` | `#DAA520` | Luxury, premium, authority |

### Split-Complementary Palettes

**Rule:** A base color plus the two colors adjacent to its complement. Less tension than full complementary, but still high contrast.

**When to use:** When you want complementary contrast but softer and more versatile. Great for multi-element designs.

| Base | Split 1 | Split 2 | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| `#1565C0` (Blue) | `#FF8F00` (Amber) | `#FF3D00` (Deep Orange) | Vibrant, trustworthy with warmth |
| `#2E7D32` (Green) | `#E91E63` (Pink) | `#FF6F00` (Orange) | Natural with pop |
| `#7B1FA2` (Purple) | `#FFD600` (Yellow) | `#76FF03` (Lime) | Creative, energetic |
| `#D32F2F` (Red) | `#00BFA5` (Teal) | `#2196F3` (Blue) | Bold with cool balance |

### Triadic Palettes

**Rule:** Three colors equally spaced on the color wheel (120 degrees apart). Vibrant, balanced.

**When to use:** Playful, dynamic content. Gaming, entertainment, youth brands. Use 60-30-10 ratio strictly.

| Color 1 | Color 2 | Color 3 | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| `#D32F2F` (Red) | `#1565C0` (Blue) | `#FDD835` (Yellow) | Primary, energetic, playful |
| `#FF6D00` (Orange) | `#7B1FA2` (Purple) | `#00C853` (Green) | Vibrant, creative |
| `#E91E63` (Pink) | `#00BCD4` (Cyan) | `#CDDC39` (Lime) | Modern, lively |
| `#FF5722` (Deep Orange) | `#3F51B5` (Indigo) | `#8BC34A` (Light Green) | Dynamic, balanced |

### Deriving a Video Palette from a Brand's Primary Color

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Start with the brand's primary color** (e.g., `#1565C0`)
2. **Background gradient:** Use a dark shade of the primary (`#0D47A1` to `#1A237E`) or a complementary dark (`#1A1A2E`)
3. **Text color:** White `#FFFFFF` or near-white `#F5F5F5` on dark backgrounds; dark `#1A1A2A` on light backgrounds
4. **Accent color:** Use the complementary (`#FF8F00`) or split-complementary for CTAs
5. **Secondary color:** Use an analogous neighbor for supporting elements
6. **Apply 60-30-10:** 60% background (gradient), 30% secondary/text areas, 10% accent (CTA, highlights)

## 3. Gradient Design for Video Backgrounds

### Types of Gradients

| Type | Description | CSS Syntax | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Linear** | Color transition along a straight line at specified angle | `linear-gradient(135deg, #color1, #color2)` | Most video backgrounds, versatile |
| **Radial** | Color radiates outward from a center point | `radial-gradient(circle, #color1, #color2)` | Spotlight effects, focus on center content |
| **Conic** | Color sweeps around a center point like a pie chart | `conic-gradient(from 0deg, #color1, #color2)` | Creative effects, wheel animations |
| **Mesh** | Multi-point color blending across a surface | Multiple layered radial gradients | Complex, organic backgrounds |

### Best Gradient Angles

| Angle | Direction | Effect | When to Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0deg** | Bottom to top | Uplifting, ascending | Growth messaging, positive outcomes |
| **45deg** | Bottom-left to top-right | Dynamic, forward-moving | Progress, journey, forward momentum |
| **90deg** | Left to right | Reading flow direction, transition | Before/after, timeline, progression |
| **135deg** | Top-left to bottom-right | Most natural, "light source" feel | Default choice, most versatile |
| **180deg** | Top to bottom | Grounding, settling, stability | Headers, hero sections, calm content |
| **270deg** | Right to left | Against reading flow, attention-grabbing | Pattern interrupts, contrast |

**Default recommendation:** Use `135deg` for most video backgrounds. It mimics natural top-left light source and creates comfortable diagonal flow.

### Color Stop Rules

| Stops | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **2-color** | Simplest, cleanest. Use for bold contrast or smooth blend. | `#667eea, #764ba2` |
| **3-color** | Middle color should bridge the two ends. Avoid muddy middle. | `#667eea, #8B5CF6, #764ba2` |
| **4-color** | Use sparingly. Each stop should be a logical step. Keep stops evenly spaced or use weighted positioning. | `#0F2027, #203A43, #2C5364, #4CA1AF` |
| **5+ color** | Almost always too many. Creates muddy, unfocused backgrounds. Avoid unless creating rainbow/spectrum effects. | Avoid |

### Gradient Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Muddy middle** | Complementary colors mix to gray/brown in the middle zone | Add a bridging third color, or use analogous colors instead |
| **Clashing stops** | Too-different hues create visual noise | Keep stops within 60-120 degrees on color wheel |
| **Too many colors** | Loses focus, looks like a test pattern | Maximum 3 stops for most uses |
| **Equal saturation everywhere** | Flat, no depth, no focal point | Vary saturation: darker at edges, lighter at center |
| **Neon on neon** | Eye strain, illegible text on top | Pair neon with dark or desaturated tones |
| **No contrast with text** | Text disappears into gradient | Ensure darkest gradient stop has 4.5:1+ contrast with text |

### Dark vs. Light Gradients

| Type | When to Use | Text Color | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dark gradients** (`#0a0a0a` to `#1a1a2e`) | Nighttime, premium, cinematic, tech, gaming | White `#FFFFFF` or light accent | Serious, immersive, premium |
| **Light gradients** (`#f5f5f5` to `#e8e8e8`) | Daytime, clean, wellness, minimal, professional | Dark `#1A1A2A` or brand color | Fresh, open, approachable |
| **Medium gradients** (`#2d3436` to `#636e72`) | Versatile, editorial, storytelling | White or bright accent | Balanced, sophisticated |

## 4. Color Grading & Film Looks

### What Color Grading Is

Color grading adjusts the colors, contrast, and tonal balance of video or motion graphics to create a specific mood or visual style. In video ads, color grading is applied via:
- Overlay layers with blend modes and opacity
- Color tint adjustments to shadows, midtones, and highlights
- Saturation and contrast manipulation

### Film Look Recipes

#### Teal & Orange (Blockbuster Cinematic)

The most popular color grade in cinema. Works because teal and orange are complementary colors with the highest contrast between exposure values of any complementary pair. Skin tones sit in the orange spectrum, so teal shadows make subjects pop.

| Element | Hex Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow tint | `#008080` | Push shadows toward teal/cyan |
| Highlight tint | `#FF8C42` | Push highlights toward warm orange |
| Midtone base | `#1A3A3A` | Desaturated teal midtones |
| Skin tone target | `#E8A87C` | Warm, natural skin rendering |
| Overall overlay | `#006666` at 15-25% opacity, `multiply` blend | Teal wash on entire frame |
| Contrast | Lift blacks to `#0A1A1A`, crush whites to `#F0E0D0` | Slightly faded blacks, warm whites |

**Implementation as overlay:**
```
Layer 1: Solid `#005F73` — blend: multiply — opacity: 20%
Layer 2: Solid `#FF8C42` — blend: soft-light — opacity: 15%
Result: Teal-shifted shadows, warm highlights
```

#### Warm Vintage / Film Stock

| Element | Hex Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow tint | `#4A3728` | Warm brown shadows |
| Highlight tint | `#FFF4DA` | Creamy, warm highlights |

| Fade overlay | `#F5F0E6` at 5-8% opacity, `screen` blend | Lifted, faded blacks |

### Blend Mode Reference for Color Grading

| Blend Mode | Effect | Use For |
|---|---|---|
| **multiply** | Darkens. White becomes transparent, black stays. Result always darker than base. | Adding shadow tints, darkening overlays |
| **screen** | Lightens. Black becomes transparent, white stays. Result always lighter than base. | Adding highlight tints, glow effects, lifting blacks |
| **overlay** | Combines multiply and screen. Dark areas get darker, light areas get lighter. | Overall contrast boost, midtone punch |
| **soft-light** | Gentler version of overlay. Subtle tinting. | Subtle color washes, gentle grading |
| **color** | Applies hue and saturation of overlay while preserving luminosity of base. | Color tinting without affecting brightness |
| **saturation** | Applies saturation of overlay to base. | Desaturation or saturation boost |
| **hue** | Applies hue of overlay while preserving saturation and luminosity of base. | Hue shifts without affecting contrast |
| **luminosity** | Applies brightness of overlay while preserving hue and saturation of base. | Contrast adjustments without color shifts |

### Opacity Guidelines

| Effect Goal | Opacity Range |
|---|---|
| **Subtle tint** | 5-10% |
| **Noticeable color grade** | 15-25% |
| **Strong color grade** | 30-45% |
| **Dominant color wash** | 50-70% |
| **Near-solid overlay** | 75-90% |

## 5. Text Color & Background Contrast

### Contrast Ratio Requirements

| Level | Ratio | Normal Text | Large Text (18pt+ / 14pt bold+) | Use For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WCAG AA** | 4.5:1 | Required | 3:1 required | Minimum for most text in video |
| **WCAG AAA** | 7:1 | Required | 4.5:1 required | Ideal for key messaging, CTAs |
| **Video minimum** | 5:1+ recommended | All key text | 4:1 minimum | Account for compression artifacts |

**Video note:** Video compression degrades contrast. Always design for higher contrast than static images. Target 7:1+ for critical text.

### Best Text Colors on Dark Backgrounds

| Background | Best Text Colors | Contrast Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| `#000000` (Black) | `#FFFFFF` (White) — 21:1 | Perfect |

## 6. Color Transitions & Animation

### Smooth Color Interpolation

| Method | Description | Implementation Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **RGB interpolation** | Linear blend between RGB values | Simple but can produce muddy midpoints between distant hues |
| **HSL interpolation** | Blend via hue, saturation, lightness | Better perceptual smoothness; avoids gray midpoints |
| **OKLCH interpolation** | Perceptually uniform color space | Best quality; no unexpected dark/bright midpoints |
| **Through a midpoint** | Add a bridge color between distant hues | `interpolateColors(frame, [0, 15, 30], ['#FF0000', '#FF8800', '#FFFF00'])` |

**Rule:** For colors more than 90 degrees apart on the color wheel, always add a midpoint color to avoid muddy transitions.

### Color Transition Timing

| Speed | Duration | Frame Count (30fps) | Effect | Use Case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Instant** | 0-0.1s | 0-3 frames | Jarring cut, pattern interrupt | Beat drops, scene changes, shock reveals |
| **Fast** | 0.2-0.5s | 6-15 frames | Energetic, snappy | CTA reveals, text color changes, highlights |
| **Medium** | 0.5-1.5s | 15-45 frames | Smooth, intentional | Background mood shifts, scene transitions |
| **Slow** | 2-4s | 60-120 frames | Gradual, cinematic | Ambient mood changes, sunrise/sunset effects |
| **Ultra-slow** | 5-10s+ | 150-300+ frames | Barely perceptible, atmospheric | Looping ambient backgrounds, meditation content |

### Specific Transition Patterns

#### Hue Rotation
Shift the hue of a color while maintaining saturation and lightness.

```
Start: #FF0000 (red, hue 0)
→ 90deg: #80FF00 (chartreuse, hue 90)
→ 180deg: #00FFFF (cyan, hue 180)
→ 270deg: #8000FF (purple, hue 270)
→ 360deg: #FF0000 (back to red)
```

**Use case:** Spectrum sweeps, loading animations, creative reveals. Duration: 3-8 seconds for full rotation.

#### Saturation Transition (Desaturated to Color Reveal)

| Phase | Saturation | Hex Example (blue base) | Frames |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desaturated | 0% | `#808080` (gray) | 0-30 |
| Emerging | 30% | `#6680B3` | 30-45 |
| Half color | 60% | `#4D66CC` | 45-55 |
| Full color | 100% | `#3355FF` | 55-60 |

**Use case:** Product reveals, dramatic before/after. Common in Apple-style ads. Duration: 0.5-2 seconds.

#### Temperature Shift (Cool to Warm)

| Phase | Description | Overlay Color | Opacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool start | Blue-teal atmosphere | `#1B3A4B` multiply | 25% |
| Transition | Neutral midpoint | Fade overlay to 0% | Linear fade |
| Warm end | Orange-amber atmosphere | `#FF8C42` soft-light | 20% |

**Duration:** 1-3 seconds. **Use case:** Mood shifts, problem-to-solution transitions.

#### Warm to Cool (Reverse)

| Phase | Overlay Color | Opacity |
|---|---|---|
| Warm start | `#FF6B35` soft-light | 20% |
| Transition | Crossfade | Linear |
| Cool end | `#006064` multiply | 20% |

#### Color Pulse (Rhythmic Breathing)

A color oscillates between two states in a repeating cycle.

```
Base color: #6200EA (deep purple)
Pulse color: #B388FF (light purple)
Cycle: 0.5-1.5 seconds per pulse
Easing: ease-in-out (sinusoidal)
Pattern: [base → pulse → base → pulse → ...]
```

**Implementation:** `interpolateColors(frame, [0, 15, 30], [baseColor, pulseColor, baseColor])` with looping.

**Variations:**
| Name | Base | Pulse | Speed | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heartbeat | `#D32F2F` | `#FF8A80` | 0.8s cycle | Urgent, alive |
| Calm Breath | `#1565C0` | `#90CAF9` | 2s cycle | Peaceful, meditative |
| Neon Flicker | `#00E5FF` | `#18FFFF` | 0.3s cycle | Electric, digital |
| Warm Glow | `#FF8F00` | `#FFE082` | 1.5s cycle | Cozy, inviting |

#### Rainbow / Spectrum Sweep

Sequential hue progression across the full spectrum:

```
Frame 0:   #FF0000 (Red)
Frame 10:  #FF8800 (Orange)
Frame 20:  #FFFF00 (Yellow)
Frame 30:  #00FF00 (Green)
Frame 40:  #0088FF (Blue)
Frame 50:  #8800FF (Indigo)
Frame 60:  #FF00FF (Violet)
Frame 70:  #FF0000 (Back to red, loop)
```

**Duration:** 2-4 seconds for full sweep. **Use case:** Pride content, celebration, party/event, creative reveals.

### Syncing Color Transitions to Music Beats

| Technique | Implementation | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Beat-locked color change** | Instant color swap exactly on each beat (every 0.5s at 120 BPM) | Energetic, dance, EDM-style ads |
| **Beat-triggered pulse** | Color pulse starts on each beat, fades between beats | Rhythmic, musical content |
| **Downbeat gradient shift** | Gradient angle or colors shift on each measure (every 2s at 120 BPM) | Longer-form content with musical structure |
| **Crescendo saturation** | Saturation builds from 30% to 100% over musical build-up | Emotional reveals, product launches |
| **Drop to color** | Desaturated throughout verse, full color explosion at beat drop | Maximum impact reveals |

**BPM to frame mapping (at 30fps):**
| BPM | Frames per Beat | Frames per Measure (4/4) |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | 30 | 120 |
| 90 | 20 | 80 |
| 120 | 15 | 60 |
| 140 | ~13 | ~51 |
| 160 | ~11 | ~45 |

## 10. Color Do's and Don'ts

### The 60-30-10 Rule Applied to Video Frames

| Proportion | Role | Example Application |
|---|---|---|
| **60% — Dominant** | Background gradient or solid | The gradient background filling the entire frame |
| **30% — Secondary** | Supporting elements, text areas, secondary graphics | Text blocks, product images, UI elements |
| **10% — Accent** | CTAs, highlights, attention focal points | CTA button, price highlight, key metric |

**Implementation for video ads:**
- Frame background (gradient) = 60%
- Text and supporting visuals = 30%
- CTA button and key highlights = 10%

### Common Color Mistakes in Video Ads

| Mistake | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **Too many colors** | Visual chaos, no hierarchy, brand dilution | Limit to 3-4 colors max per frame |
| **Low contrast text** | Illegible, accessibility failure, message lost | Maintain 5:1+ contrast ratio; use shadows/pills |
| **Complementary gradient** | Muddy brown/gray middle zone | Use analogous colors for gradients, or add bridging stops |
| **Oversaturated everything** | Eye fatigue, nothing stands out, cheap look | Use saturation strategically — high for accents only |
| **Ignoring dark mode** | Content blends into feed or glows painfully | Test on `#000000` and `#FFFFFF` backgrounds |
| **Clashing brand + platform** | Brand colors fighting platform UI | Adapt brand palette to work with platform context |
| **Random color choices** | No emotional coherence, confusing messaging | Choose colors for their psychological effect |
| **Same weight for all colors** | Flat, boring, no visual hierarchy | Apply 60-30-10 rule strictly |
| **Neon text on neon background** | Illegible, headache-inducing | Neon accents only — pair with dark or neutral base |
| **White text on light gradient** | Invisible text, wasted messaging | Place text on darkest portion, or add dark overlay behind text |

### Colors That Fight Each Other

These combinations create visual vibration, eye strain, or mud when placed adjacent:

| Combination | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Red `#FF0000` + Green `#00FF00` (pure) | Christmas cliche, vibrates at equal luminance | Desaturate one: `#C62828` + `#81C784` |
| Red `#FF0000` + Blue `#0000FF` (pure) | Vibrates, hard to read | Shift: `#D32F2F` + `#42A5F5` |
| Orange `#FF6600` + Purple `#6600FF` | Muddy overlap zone | Use as split-complementary with bridge color |
| Neon Pink `#FF00FF` + Neon Green `#00FF00` | Maximum eye strain | Lower saturation on one, or separate with dark |
| Yellow `#FFFF00` + White `#FFFFFF` | No contrast, invisible | Yellow on dark only, or use gold `#DAA520` |

### When to Break Color Rules

| Situation | Rule to Break | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| **Pattern interrupt** | Use clashing, vibrating colors | Involuntary attention — viewer cannot look away |
| **Urgency/flash sale** | Oversaturate reds and yellows | Mimics danger signals, triggers urgency |
| **Creative/art brand** | Use 5+ colors, break 60-30-10 | Communicates creativity and rule-breaking itself |
| **Youth/Gen-Z** | Neon-on-neon, acid colors | Matches audience aesthetic expectations |
| **Irony/humor** | Deliberately "bad" color combos | Self-aware design reads as clever, not incompetent |
